# Care Guides

## Outdoor Cushions and Performance Fabric (Sunbrella)
Sunbrella performance fabrics and products are easy to clean and built for long-lasting durability - standing up to all of life's toughest messes. Sunbrella fabric is resistant to fading, UV rays, stains, water, mold, and mildew.

## Clean Sunbrella Upholstery
The most important rule with keeping sunbrella upholstery clean is to clean spills and dirt quickly. You should blot (not rub) liquid spills with a clean, dry cloth. For oil-based spills, apply an absorbent such as cornstarch, then remove with a straight edge. Spray on a mild cleaning solution of soap and water or use Sunbrella Clean Multi Purpose Fabric Cleaner. Rinse the fabric thoroughly to remove all soap residue, then let it air dry. If the prior attempts do not work then you can try other options. For handwashing sunbrella upholstery you must soak fabric in a solution of 1/4 cup mild soap per gallon of lukewarm water or use Sunbrella Clean Multi-Purpose Fabric Cleaner. Use a sponge or soft bristle brush to lightly agitate the stain, then rinse thoroughly to remove all soap residue and allow fabric to air dry. For machine washing, some removable casings can be washed in the washing machine, however consult the furniture manufacturer before removing the casing as cushion construction may vary. To wash, first close all zippers, machine wash in cold water on the delicate cycle with normal amounts of mild laundry detergent. For severe mold or mildew, add 1 cup of bleach, then allow the fabric to air dry. For Cleaning Non-Removable Fabric, prepare a solution of 1/4 cup mild soap per gallon of lukewarm water, or use Sunbrella Clean Multi-Purpose Fabric Cleaner. Use a soft bristle brush to clean, allowing the cleaning solution to soak into the fabric. Rinse thoroughly to remove all residue and allow fabric to air dry. For tough, colored stains, use Sunbrella Erase Stain & Spot Remover. For the best results when cleaning upholstered pieces, clean the entire surface area of the fabric, from seam to seam, instead of rubbing the fabric in a circular motion on the spill. This method, along with an extractor, may be required in order to avoid water rings and residue. For Oil-Based Stains, if a stubborn oil-based stain persists after cleaning, use Sunbrella Extract Oil Based Stain Remover or treat with a strong degreaser and rinse thoroughly. For Mold and Mildew, sunbrella fabrics do not promote mildew growth, however mildew may grow on dirt and other foreign substances if not removed from the fabric. To remove mold and mildew, first prepare a solution of 1 cup of bleach and 1/4 cup mild soap per gallon of water or use Sunbrella Renew Mold & Mildew Stain Remover. Then spray on entire area and allow to soak into the fabric for 15 minutes. Afterwards, clean entire surface area with a sponge, clean towel or very soft bristle brush, then rinse thoroughly to remove all soap residue and air dry. Bleach quantities can be increased with severe mold or mildew growth.

## Clean Sunbrella Slings
One of the best ways to care for your sunbrella sling, in order to reduce the need for deep cleaning, is to rinse regularly with clean water to help prevent dirt from becoming stuck in the fabric. For General or Light Cleaning, rinse with clean water, then prepare a solution of 1/4 cup mild soap per gallon of lukewarm water or use Sunbrella Clean Multi-Purpose Fabric Cleaner. Afterwards, use a soft bristle brush to clean the fabric, then rinse thoroughly to remove all soap residue and allow fabric to air dry; repeat as necessary. For Heavy Cleaning For Stubborn Spills and Mildew, sunbrella fabrics do not promote mildew growth, however, mildew may grow on dirt and other foreign substances if not removed from the fabric. To remove mold and mildew, first prepare a solution of 1 cup of bleach and 1/4 cup mild soap per gallon of water or use Sunbrella Renew Mold & Mildew Stain Remover. Then spray on entire area and allow the solution to sit for 15 minutes, continue to spray area if it begins to dry too quickly; Clean entire surface area with a sponge, clean towel or very soft bristle brush. Afterwards, rinse thoroughly to remove all soap residue. Finally, Air dry. For severe mold/mildew growth, bleach quantities may be increased. It is best to clean the entire surface area of the fabric to avoid water rings and residue. For Oil-Based Spills, immediately apply an absorbent such as cornstarch directly on the oil-based spill, then let it sit for 10 minutes and remove the excess using a straight edge or ruler. This helps remove the spill and prevents it from being pressed into the fabric. Then proceed with the cleaning process. For tough stains, use Sunbrella Extract Oil Based Stain Remover or treat with a strong degreaser and rinse thoroughly.

## Clean Sunbrella Shade Fabrics (For Umbrellas)
One of the best ways to keep Sunbrella fabrics looking good is to hose down the fabrics on a monthly basis with clean water. This helps prevent dirt from embedding into the fabric and eliminates the need for more frequent and vigorous cleaning, in most environments, a thorough cleaning will be needed every two to three years. When it is time to clean, the fabric can be cleaned while still on an awning frame or, size permitting, they can be removed to aid in the cleaning of stubborn spills or for professional cleaning. For General or Light Cleaning while on awning frame, brush off loose dirt, hose down, prepare a cleaning solution of water and mild soap or use Sunbrella Clean Multi Purpose Fabric Cleaner, then use a soft bristle brush to clean. Afterwards, allow the cleaning solution to soak into the fabric, rinse thoroughly until all soap residue is removed, air dry, and depending on the age of the fabric you may not need a retreatment. If stubborn stains persist, you can use a diluted bleach/soap mixture or Sunbrella Renew Mold & Mildew Stain Remover for spot cleaning of mildew, roof run-off or other similar spills. For Heavy Cleaning for Stubborn Stains and Mildew, Sunbrella fabrics do not promote mildew growth, but mildew may grow on dirt or other foreign substances that are not removed from the fabric. To clean stubborn stains, first prepare a solution of 1 cup of bleach and 1/4 cup mild soap per gallon of clean water or use Sunbrella Renew Mold & Mildew Stain Remover, then allow mixture to soak into fabric for up to 15 minutes. Afterwards, clean with soft bristle brush (Note: this step should be avoided when cleaning the coated side [back side] of Sunbrella Plus), rinse thoroughly until all soap residue is removed, air dry, and repeat if necessary. Retreatment of fabric for water and stain resistance will be necessary after using a bleach solution. Note: Use caution when cleaning the coated (underside) of Sunbrella Plus with a bleach solution. The coated side of the fabric should only be sprayed and rinsed thoroughly. For Oil-Based Spills, immediately apply an absorbent such as cornstarch directly on to the oil-based spill. Let it sit for 10 minutes then remove the excess using a straight edge or ruler; this helps remove the spill and prevents it from being pressed into the fabric, then proceed with the cleaning process. For tough stains use Sunbrella Extract Oil Based Stain Remover or treat with a strong degreaser and rinse thoroughly. For retreating Sunbrella fabrics, Sunbrella fabrics are engineered to provide long-term performance. After repeated thorough cleanings, or years of use and exposure, you may choose to refresh your fabrics with an appropriate retreating agent. We recommend Sunbrella Restore Fabric Protector & Repellent. Applying a retreatment involves cleaning the Sunbrella fabric using one of the cleaning methods outlined earlier, allow sunbrella to completely air dry, then apply retreatment in a well-ventilated area following instructions on the container.

## How to Clean & Sanitize POLYWOOD Furniture
Supplies needed: Dry vacuum cleaner, mild dish soap, plastic bucket, (optional) liquid bleach, wash cloth, soft-bristle brush, garden hose. Instructions: Vacuum off loose dirt, mix dish soap with warm water in a bucket, then add bleach if you want to sanitize (1 part bleach to 2 parts water). Then wipe the furniture with a washcloth dipped in the soapy water, let it sit a few minutes, then scrub with a soft-bristle brush. Finally, rinse with clean water and let it air-dry. Tips: Wear chemical-resistant gloves if you plan to use bleach, for a natural bleach alternative, try white vinegar and baking soda, and avoid using abrasive cleaning or polishing scrubbers (such as eraser pads) on lumber.

## How to Pressure Wash POLYWOOD Furniture
Supplies needed: A pressure washer (rated no higher than 1,500 psi) equipped with a 40-degree fan tip. Instructions: Spray all areas of your furniture with slow, even sweeps, keeping the nozzle about 8 inches away from the surface. If any stubborn spots remain, follow the Clean & Sanitize steps to remove them. Tips: Always keep the pressure washer's wand moving, spraying one spot continuously can cause gouges and other damage. Wear goggles, gloves, a facemask, boots, and ear protection when using a pressure washer.

## How to Clean POLYWOOD Stainless Steel Hardware
All POLYWOOD furniture hardware (screws, nuts, bolts, and other fasteners) is made using marine-grade 18-8 stainless steel with an AlloChrome 3200+ finish and cosmetic coating. Supplies needed: Mild dish soap, plastic bucket, soft-bristle brush, garden hose, towel. Instructions: Mix soap and warm water in the bucket, then apply the soapy mixture to the hardware with the brush, finally rinse thoroughly and towel-dry. Salt Air Tips: Apply a color-appropriate applicance touch-up paint (available at your local hardware store). Dab on a coat of clear car wax. Also, if the hardware comes in contact with salt water, rinse it right away with fresh water and towel dry.

## How to Clean POLYWOOD Cushions & Pillows
Supplies Needed: (optional) dry vacuum, garden hose, mild dish soap, plastic bucket, washcloth. Instructions: Brush, rinse, or vacuum up loose dirt and debris, then mix dish soap with warm water (1/4 cup soap per gallon) in the bucket. Afterwards, apply the mixture to the material with a clean cloth, and finally rinse thoroughly and let your cushions air-dry. Tips: Prop cushions on their sides to help them dry faster. If possible, flip your cushions periodically to even out the wear on each side. Store the patio cushions in a dry spot, such as a shed, garage, or console table, when not in use. 

## How to Clean POLYWOOD Sling Fabric
Supplies Needed: Whisk broom, Mild Dish Soap, Liquid Bleach, Plastic Bucket, Sponge or Washcloth, Garden Hose. Instructions: Brush off loose dirt, mix dish soap and bleach with water (1 Tbsp soap + 2 Tbsp bleach per gallon), then scrub the fabric with the solution and rinse well before letting it air-dry. Avoid Oil-Based Products because oil-based products can damage outdoor sling fabrics and make them age faster, if direct contact occurs you must clean it right away. Supplies Needed to clean oil-based spills: Paper towel, cornstarch, dry vacuum cleaner or straight edge. Instructions: Blot any excess oil with paper towels (don't rub), and apply cornstarch to the stain before letting it sit for 10 to 15 minutes, then remove the cornstarch using the dry vacuum or straight edge. Finally, repeat the process until all the oil has been absorbed.

## How to Deep Clean POLYWOOD Patio Umbrellas
Supplies Needed: Dry Vacuum cleaner fitted with a brush attachment, garden hose, plastic bucket, mild dish soap, bleach, soft-bristle brush. Instructions: Open your umbrella and lay it on its side, vacuum up dust and loose dirt, then hose off both sides of the canopy with clean water. Afterwards, mix warm water, 1 cup bleach, and 1/4 cup soap per gallon in a bucket, scrub the soapy solution onto the fabric and pole with the soft-bristle, then let it sit for 15 minutes. Finally, rinse thoroughly and leave the umbrella open to air-dry. NOTE: Only use this method on bleach-cleanable performance fabrics. Before trying this approach, check with your umbrella's manufacturer to see if it's safe to clean with bleach.

## How to Clean POLYWOOD Synthetic Wicker
Supplies Needed: Dry vacuum cleaner or paintbrush, mild dish soap, plastic bucket, soft-bristle brush, toothbrush, towel. Instructions: Remove cushions first, vacuum or brush off debris, mix soap and water in the bucket, scrub the soapy water over all surfaces (using a toothbrush for tight spots), and finally rinse with clean water and dry with a towel.

## How to Clean POLYWOOD Aluminum Furniture
Supplies Needed: Garden hose, mild dish soap, bucket of warm water, soft cloth or sponge, microfiber towel. Instructions: Remove loose dirt, pollen, and dust with the garden hose, then mix soap and water in the bucket. Afterwards, use the cloth and soapy water to thoroughly clean all surfaces, finally rinse with clean water and towel-dry immediately after. Tips: For stubborn stains, apply a 1:1 solution of water and white vinegar to the aluminum after washing with soapy water, then let it sit for a few minutes before rinsing and drying. Avoid using abrasive brushes or pads with harsh chemicals, such as bleach or ammonia-based cleaners, which can damage the aluminum and its protective powder coating.

## How to Clean Less Often
Store outdoor furniture and cushions in your shed or garage in the off-season. Invest in durable covers that protect your furniture when not in use. If possible, keep furniture and cushions out of direct sunlight to reduce fading. Spray outdoor cushions and umbrellas with fabric protectant that repels water, stains, and mildew. Protect aluminum deck furniture with a coat of car wax, exterior paint, or a silicone spray like WD-40. Follow the manufacturer's care instructions, other methods may cause damage or require extra cleaning.

## How to Clean American Fire Grills Products
When cleaning an American Fire Grills product, the first thing to do is to remove ash and debris. To remove the ash and debris, wait until the metal or cast-iron is completely cool (at least 2 hours), then use a fireplace shovel or brush to clear out accumulated ash from the grate gaps to maintain proper airflow. Later, you should wipe down surfaces by using a stiff, dry brush or a damp cloth with plain water. When wiping down surfaces on the grill you should avoid harsh chemical detergents or chemical accelerants that can leave toxic residues or taint future fires. Finally you should dry the grill thoroughly, you should dry the iron and steel components completely after cleaning to prevent surface rust. To prevent rust you should be proactive about scrubbing any minor rust spots that develop on the steel or cast iron. You should scrub away rust spots using a wire brush and applying a thin layer of high-temperature oil or high-temperature flat-black paint if necessary. To protect your grill the best you should get a grill cover or store the grill inside during the off season or extended rainy/stormy conditions.

## Bernhardt Outdoor Care & Cleaning
To maintain structure and evenly distribute wear, frequently reshape and rotate back cushions and pillows. Occasionally vacuum your furniture fabrics and cushions using upholstery and crevice attachments to remove all dirt and debris before it becomes embedded in the fabric. For light cleaning: Lightly brush off or blot any loose dirt and light stains, then blot or mist spray with a lukewarm water/mild detergent solution (avoid oversaturating), finally rinse with water until clean and allow to dry thoroughly. For stubborn stains, mold, and mildew: Substitute the water/detergent solution used in the light cleaning method with a bleach solution of 30 parts lukewarm water to 1 part liquid bleach. We do not recommend removing fabric covers from the cushion core. While Bernhardt fabrics are mildew resistant, spores that create mold and mildew thrive in environments where there is dirt and moisture. To deter buildup, keep cushions clean and free of debris. Always remove wet cushions from furniture frames and allow to dry. If upholstery or cushions require extensive cleaning, please contact a professional. Always ensure your furniture and fabrics are clean and dry before covering and storing. 

## Caring for Bernhardt Cushions
Exterior Plush Cushions: If moisture does penetrate the fabric, open the zipper of the cover and dry the layer between the fabric and the sealed cushion using a towel. Excess water will continue to drain through the mesh panel on the underside.
Reticulated Foam Cushions: The open cell construction of this cushion allows water to drain through when wet. For saturated cushions, open the zipper of the cover and stand on end with zipper facing downward to allow excess water to drain through.
Back Cushions and Throw Pillows: Open the zipper of the cover and stand on end with zipper facing downward to allow excess water to drain through.

## Cleaning and Maintaining Ebel Furniture
A consistent schedule of cleaning the surface will help remove dirt, grime, and other surface build-ups that can help keep your furniture looking new. Over time, powder-coated finishes exposed to the elements may show signs of weathering such as reduced gloss and slight color loss. Use a non-abrasive soap or household cleaner and wipe aluminum, woven, or Reserve pieces with a soft cloth. Furniture can be rinsed with hose water. We do not recommend using chemical cleaners such as acetone, alcohol, mineral spirits, or lacquer thinners. Any type of chemical that is harmful to the powder coat surface should be removed or cleaned immediately. Avoid using pressure washers.

## Cleaning Ebel Cushions
Be sure to remove any loose dirt first by brushing off loose dirt or shaking them off. Test a small, inconspicuous area to make sure the cleaner will not affect the color of the fabric. Spray a household cleaner on the fabric and use a soft bristle brush to clean. Rinse thoroughly to remove all of the cleaners. Air-dry the cushion faster by unzipping it. Never put cushions or cushion covers in the dryer.

All firepits are CSA approved. If your firepit is directly exposed to precipitation, a cover is recommended when the fire pit is not in use. Ebel Fire Pits are meant to be used exclusively outdoors. Do not store or use gasoline, or other flammable vapors or liquids in the vicinity of this or any other appliance. This appliance must be supervised at all times. Never use this appliance in an enclosed space at any time.

## Lloyd Flanders Care and Maintenance
How to clean Lloyd Flander's cushions: Cushions are upholstered in Sunbrella or similar material (solution-dyed acrylic fabrics) so cleaning should be taken with one cup of chlorine bleach diluted in one gallon of water with a small amount of mild detergent. Do not use bleach on printed fabrics. Test the cleaning solution in a small inconspicuous area first before trying other parts of the chair. Saturate a cloth or soft brush with the solution, then scrub the entire cushion and rinse well with clean water and let air dry. Printed fabrics may be scrubbed with a solution of mild detergent and warm water. Test in a small inconspicuous area first. Saturate a cloth or soft brush with the solution, then begin scrubbing the cushions, one small area at a time. Rinse well with water, and let cushions air dry. Printed polyester fabric may be scrubbed with a solution of mild detergent and warm water. Do not use bleach on printed polyester fabrics. Rinse and air dry. Do not put cushion covers in dryer or send out to dry cleaners, because this will void the warranty. To remove mildew from cushions you should mix one gallon of distilled water with one cup of chlorine bleach, then add a squirt of dish detergent (check detergent label to make sure it does not have ammonia). Do not put bleach on printed fabrics. We do not recommend pressure washing Lloyd Flanders furniture, instead you should use a soft brush or sponge with an appropriate cleaning solution. For prevention, you can purchase a furniture cover from Treasure Garden to ensure that the furniture has a break from weathering.

## OW Lee Porcelain Tile Tops Care
Porcelain tiles are made from porcelain clays which are denser, finer grained clay which makes them stronger once fired than non-porcelain ceramic tiles. Moreover, porcelain tiles have a low water absorption rate (less than 0.5%) which makes them very frost resistant. You should keep porcelain tops free of dust or dirt through regular cleaning. Clean the porcelain top using mild liquid soap, water, and a clean soft rag; finally you should follow with rinsing the soap residue with clean water. For tougher stains such as long-term dirt build-up, food stains, and more, you should use a ph-balanced cleaner on porcelain. Always store the tops during extended periods of unuse.

## OW Lee Dekton Care and Maintenance
Routine Care for Dekton involves very little maintenance. You only have to wipe your Dekton surface using a non-abrasive sponge with soap and water on a regular basis; doing so will keep it looking young and shiny for years. In addition, any pH balanced general-purpose cleaner can be used (Windex, Dish soap, etc). For difficult spills, wipe the surface with Pot and Pan soap, then scrub with a Scotch Brite pad, and rinse with warm water. For stains that harden as they dry (food, gum, nail polish, or paint) you should clean by gently scraping the stain off of the surface using a blade or putty knife. Afterwards, clean the area using warm water and soap. Gray marks left by the razor can be wiped away with soap and water or glass cooktop cleaner. Dekton's advanced technology makes its surface extremely resistant to damaging chemicals, a couple good cleaners to use for difficult spots include:
For Wine, Coffee, Food Stains, Fruit Juices use common alkaline detergents or oxidants (Dish Soap, Formula 409, Windex, or diluted bleach in water).
For Wax, Shoe Polish, Rubber Marks, Dyes, Markers, Inks, and Adhesives use Lacquer Thinner, Acetone, Rubbing Alcohol, or Mineral Spirits.
For Rust, Lime Scale, Cement, or Plaster Residue use acidic based cleaners such as vinegar and water or CLR. 
For Tar or Nicotine use solvents or oxidants.
For smears, residue, or films use the Weiman glass cooktop cleaner or the Magic glass cooktop cleaner to wipe and clean with a generous amount of water with clean rags (using a squeegee can also ease the cleaning process).
All the above cleaners should sit for 10 minutes on the stain or mark.
For Extreme Heat Protection, Dekton is extremely resistant to large fluctuations in heat and cold. For safety reasons, you should place a trivet or a hot pad directly between your hot pot/pan and the Dekton surface (just so that customers are not burned when attempting to clean). Chemicals to avoid on Dekton surfaces include Hydrofluoric Acid. 
General Precautions: Dekton is very scratch resistant but you should still use a cutting board because knives or other metal utensils can leave grey marks when cutting directly on Dekton surfaces, these marks can be cleaned, but it is better to not cause them in the first place. Moreover, Dekton can dull your utensils if they are rubbing against it.

## What Happens to Teak Overtime (Royal Teak)?
As the teak oils come out of the wood and mix with weather elements, spills, dew, etc, it can form a mold/mildew that can be cleaned easily using a Teak Cleaner. As the oils move to the surface of the teak, after the first few rainfalls there can be some "bleeding" on your teak furniture. It is recommended that you remove cushions from the furniture when it is raining until the furniture dries and to avoid placing the furniture on light colored patio surfaces. This process is completely natural and only occurs in the first few months of the weathering process.
Over time, teak will develop hairline cracks known as "checking". These will smooth themselves out as the wood ages. They can be lightly sanded with sandpaper to smooth them out faster. 
Teak naturally turns to a silver-grey if left untreated. Moist climates will cause the teak to grey faster than in drier climates. To bring teak back to its natural finish use a teak cleaner to restore the color and then a teak sealer to preserve it.

## Why Does Royal Teak Furniture Turn Grey?
Over time if left untreated, teak will turn a silver-grey color through a very natural process caused by the sunlight drying out the outer layer of the teak. As the outer layer dries out, it will oxidize and begin to fade and change colors within just a few weeks. This is only cosmetic and does not indicate damage or structural weakening of the teak. To prevent teak from greying out over time you should simply use a Teak Sealer product, which can be purchased in store at American Backyard; the Teak Sealer will seal the teak preventing the natural weathering process, keeping the teak in its natural golden-honey color. Depending on climates, you should apply a teak sealer every one to 2 years. The Teak Sealer gives UV protection, prevents mildew growth, and prevent color change in Teak. One bottle of Teak Sealer can cover 1 dining table and 8-10 chairs. Do not let the Teak Sealer come in contact with other surfaces or it may negatively affect the surface or item. Always wipe down the teak with a clean cloth before applying a sealer. 

## Can I Pressure Wash my Royal Teak Furniture?
Yes you can pressure wash teak furniture. Pressure washing is most effective on teak that has been left untreated and you would like to bring back its original color. ## Winston Cushion Fabric or Suncloth Strap Cleaning and Care
Fine outdoor fabrics are designed to endure years of exposure to sun, soil, and moisture. Light stains can be removed with a solution of mild detergent and water, then rinsed with clean water and dried thoroughly. Mildew and heavy stains can be treated with a high-quality commercial outdoor furniture cleaner and protectant. For best protection, perform this furniture maintenance two to three times a year. However, these durable materials can be safely cleaned as often as desired.

## Winston Sling Fabric or Vinyl Strap Cleaning and Care
For vinyl strap and sling surfaces, apply a quality vinyl protectant to guard against the effects of air pollution, salt air build-up, and mildew growth. Light stains can be removed with a solution of mild detergent and water, rinsed with clean water, and dried thoroughly. Mildew and heavy stains can be removed with a quality commercial outdoor furniture cleaner and protectant.

## Winston Aluminum Cleaning and Care
Wash the furniture with a solution of mild detergent and water, rinse with clean water and dry thoroughly.

## Winston HDPE All-Weather Wicker and Lumber Cleaning and Care
Light stains and soils may be removed with a solution of mild detergent and water. Apply to the surface with a soft nylon brush. Brush lightly until clean. Rinse with clean water and allow to air dry. Avoid using a cloth to wipe clean or dry as it can be abrasive.

## Winston Natural Wood Cleaning and Care
Left alone to age, the natural color of teak furniture will weather to a soft, silver grey. This process will take approximately nine months, depending on the degree of sunlight and rain exposure. Imperfections, which are inherent properties of the wood, will show over time, enhancing the beauty of the furniture. In humid or wet climates, or after rains, the wood may naturally expand and contract, causing the grain to rise. This is to be expected with exposure to the elements and will stabilize after the initial weathering occurs.
To maintain teak furniture, use a soft bristled utility brush and stroke lightly in the direction of the grain to remove surface dust. For more stubborn dirt, the wood may be washed with a mild solution of four parts laundry detergent or dishwashing soap to one part bleach in water, then rinsed thoroughly and allowed to dry completely. Oiling the wood may cause mildew or irregular coloring.

## Winston Adjustable Furniture Cleaning and Care
Care should be taken to stabilize chaises and adjustable chairs at the desired set point before reclining into the furniture. This is best done standing next to the furniture until the desired position is selected and secured. Do not adjust furniture while in use.

## Winston Winter Storage Cleaning and Care
All furniture should be stored in an upright position to allow for proper water drainage. Storing furniture upside-down may cause water to accumulate and freeze inside the frame, causing serious structural damage that is not covered by the warranty.

## Winston Care and Maintenance Notes and Warnings
Do not use any kind of wax, abrasive materials, or strong detergents on textured finishes.
Some suntan and sun block products may be harmful to weaves, cushions, mesh, and vinyl straps. Do not allow such products to come in contact with any seating surfaces to avoid deep penetrating stains, which are not covered under warranty.
Do not use bleach on printed acrylic fabrics.
Do not use solvent-type materials on woven vinyl, woven blend, or ultra woven blend fabrics as they will damage the yarns.
Do not use chlorine based cleaners.
Do not dry clean or use a washing machine or dryer.
Do not steam or use water at temperatures above 100ºF when washing.
When cleaning with anything other than clean water, always test the cleaning solution in an inconspicuous area first.
